## 1. MUST-TRY DISHES IN AGADIR / SOUSS REGION

### Seafood

**Grilled sardines** — THE signature Agadir dish. Agadir is Morocco's largest Atlantic fishing port. FreshAtlantic sardines grilled whole over charcoal with cumin, salt, lemon. Served with bread and harissa. The humble, essential dish — costs almost nothing. Avoid tourist-facing beach restaurants (fish less fresh, price 3-4× higher). Eat at the port or Talborjt.

- Price: 20-45 MAD per plate (6-8 sardines) at port/local spots; 60-100 MAD at beachfront tourist spots.

**Whole grilled fish (sole, sea bream, sea bass)** — Picked by weight off the ice at port grill stalls. Agadir's near-constant sunshine means seafood eats well year-round.

- Price: 90-180 MAD by weight (whole fish); mixed seafood platter (prawns, calamari, small fried fish) 100-150 MAD for two at port.

**Sardine tagine** — Slow-cooked sardines in tomato, preserved lemon, green olive, and herb sauce. Distinctly Moroccan, deeply flavoured. Found in traditional Moroccan restaurants throughout the city. Eat with khobz bread.

**Sardine chermoula** — Sardines marinated in fresh coriander, garlic, cumin, and lemon juice before grilling. More elaborate than simple charcoal sardines. Preferred approach in better fish restaurants.

**Langouste (spiny lobster)** — Seasonal, appears at smarter tables.

**Sea urchins** — Gathered along this coast at certain times of year.

### Moroccan staples

**Tagine** — Slow-cooked stew in a conical clay pot. The classic. Agadir/Souss variations: lamb with prunes and almonds (sweet-savory), chicken with preserved lemon and olives, vegetable tagine with carrots/zucchini/chickpeas. Argan oil is a local signature addition. Also: kefta tagine (meatballs in spiced tomato sauce, often with eggs), fish tagine with chermoula, goat tagine.

- Price: 30-90 MAD at local restaurants; 60-90 MAD fish tagine at Talborjt; 60-100 MAD at mid-range; restaurant starters 80 MAD.

**Couscous** — National dish. Wheat semolina steamed over a stew. Friday specialty in Souss. Seven-vegetable couscous (carrots, turnips, pumpkin, zucchini, eggplant, onions, celery) with meat or chicken. Also: couscous with beef & vegetables, seafood couscous, baddaz (cornmeal variation with argan oil). Souss couscous is "very varied" in vegetables — a regional distinction.

- Price: 40-80 MAD at neighbourhood restaurants; 50-80 MAD Friday couscous.

**Mechoui** — Whole lamb slow-roasted until melt-in-your-mouth tender. Celebratory dish for weddings/Eid. Seasoned with cumin and salt. Best eaten at a proper mechoui restaurant where you can watch the process.

- Price: 700-1500 MAD range (€8-18) for lamb shoulder or spit-roasted pieces.

**Tangia (bachelor's stew)** — Meat stew (lamb or beef) with turmeric, cumin, garlic, lemon pickle, sometimes ras el hanout. Traditionally oven-roasted in a clay tangia pot (formerly earth oven with wood fire ashes). A Marrakech/Agadir specialty.

**Harira** — Hearty tomato-lentil-chickpea soup with meat (beef/chicken), legumes, pasta, and vegetables. Thickened with flour, distinctive tomato-spice flavor. Traditionally eaten to break Ramadan fast but available year-round. The cheapest filling soup in town.

- Price: 4-15 MAD per bowl (soup stands 0.70 DH at Souk El Had center — yes, less than 1 dirham).

**Bissara** — Fava bean (or pea) purée, thick, warming, topped with olive oil and cumin. Popular breakfast dish.

- Price: 8-12 MAD.

**Msemen** — Flaky, square Moroccan pancake. Served with honey, butter, or spicy sauce. Breakfast staple.

- Price: 5-10 MAD each.

**Baghrir (beghrir)** — "Thousand-hole" spongy semolina pancakes. Served with melted honey and butter. Breakfast favorite.

- Price: 5-10 MAD each.

**Amlou** — Signature Souss preparation. Paste of roasted almonds, argan oil, and honey. Eaten spread on fresh bread — like almond butter with deeper complexity. The breakfast of the region. Divided opinions (irresistible vs. overwhelming) but it's the authentic local taste.

- Available at argan oil cooperatives (tasting tours), breakfast cafés, Souk El Had.

**Sellou** — Energy-rich mix of almonds, sesame seeds, toasted flour, and honey. Traditional sweet snack.

**Trid** — Layered flatbread with chicken and lentils. A Soussi regional specialty, the Souss equivalent of rfissa. Rarely appears on tourist menus — ask for it at local Talborjt restaurants.

**Berkouks** — Traditional Amazigh dish, pasta made from durum wheat semolina, served with amlou.

**Tafernout bread** — Daily bread of Souss Massa region. Served with tagines; also eaten soaked in argan oil, olive oil, or amlou.

### Drinks

**Fresh orange juice** — Everywhere. 5-10 MAD per glass (squeezed to order — avoid pre-prepared). Oranges are cheap and plentiful. Pyramid displays at juice stalls signal freshness.

**Avocado smoothie** — 10-15 MAD. Local favorite.

**Prickly pear juice (cactus fruit, season)** — Souss specialty, not found in northern Morocco.

**Mint tea** — The ritual. Loaded with sugar, fragrant, poured from height with a flourish. As much about the ritual as the taste. 5-10 MAD everywhere.

**Local beer (Casablanca Beer / Flag)** — ~35 MAD.

### Sweets / Pastries

**Chebakia** — Honey-soaked flower-shaped cookies associated with Ramadan but available year-round in Agadir.

**Kaab el ghazal (gazelle horns)** — Almond-filled pastry. At restaurants like Le Dak-Art.

**Pastilla** — Sweet/savory chicken pie. Found in bakery alleys of Souk El Had (3-5 DH a piece).

**Creme caramel** — Caramel custard. At Dar Tajine and other restaurants.

**Moroccan pastries (baklava, etc.)** — At restaurants and patisseries.

## 2. WHERE TO EAT — AREAS & VIBES

### The Port (Grill Row) — Best for fresh, cheap, authentic seafood
The working fishing port north of the beach. Strip of simple stalls displaying the day's catch on ice. Choose your fish (sardines, sole, sea bream, prawns, calamari), it's weighed, priced per kilo, grilled over charcoal, served with bread, salad, lemon at plastic-chaired tables. Cash only. Busiest and freshest at lunch (morning boats deliver by 10am). Pick a stall busy with Moroccan families. Prices: sardines 25-45 MAD/plate, full meal 60-120 MAD/person. Always fix the by-weight price BEFORE cooking. This is as close to the boat as eating gets. The most honest, essential Agadir food experience.

### Souk El Had — Best for ultra-budget, local market food
Morocco's largest covered market — 6,000 stalls in a walled compound. Food section has cooked-food stalls serving tagines, grilled meats, salads, snacks. Moroccans eat here. Open daily except Monday. Morning best for atmosphere and freshness. Specifics:
- Soup stand (Souk centre): Lentil soup 0.70 DH (!), served with hard bread for dunking.
- Tagine lunches: 40-60 DH for chicken tagine, made in plain view in a dusty clay oven. Safe and full of flavour.
- Fresh juices: Orange 8-12 DH (squeeze to order). Cane sugar as alternative.
- Sweets corner: Pastilla and pastries scattered through bakery alley, 3-5 DH each.
- Msemen with honey: 5 MAD. Brochettes with bread and harissa: 10-40 MAD.
Avoid empty stalls at peak hours. Don't eat at the restaurant near the entrance gate (Rue de Souk / Rue Mohamed Iqbal corner) — go deeper into the market.

### Talborjt neighbourhood — Best for authentic local Moroccan restaurants
The old city centre. 15 minutes from the beach but a different world. Local restaurants serving full meals for under 80 MAD. The real Agadir. Cash only at most places. Specifics:
- Jour et Nuit — Local atmosphere, budget, real Moroccan food. 40-90 MAD. Local favourite in Talborjt. Crowd: taxi drivers, shop owners, office workers. Tagine of the day always reliable. Grilled chicken with frites. Fresh juices 40-90 MAD.
- Dar Tajine — Rue 2 Mars, Agadir. Authentic. Goat tagine, couscous with seasonal vegetables and tender meat, meat tagine, spiedini di pollo, Moroccan salad (سلطة مغربيه). Creme caramel for dessert. Phone: +212 5 28 21 10 28. The tagine of the day (whatever the owner found best at market) is always reliable.
- Ibtissam restaurant — at Place Lahcen Ben Brahim Tamri (heart of Agadir for locals, with cinema, art galleries, local restaurants). Best chicken tagine.
- Snack Chiche Kabab — Budget street-food institution, 3.9/5 from 1,526 guests. Shawarma, grilled meats, Moroccan street food. 30-70 MAD. Busy with locals.
- Café Oasis — Reliable stop for stuffed msemen, paninis, grilled snacks. Very reasonable prices, excellent quality.

### Corniche (Beach Promenade) — Best for sunset views, varied menus
Long palm-lined boulevard facing the beach (4-10km). Restaurants serving international cuisine, fresh Atlantic seafood, grilled fish. 100-300 MAD per person for a main + drink. Pricier than port/Talborjt (20-40% more) but you pay for the view. Sunset terrace dinners worth the premium at least once. The most lively section is northern part between Marina and the 'I Love Agadir' sign — bars, restaurants concentrated there. Beach cafés also serve snacks, juice, mint tea.

### Marina (Port de Plaisance) — Best for upscale, smart dining
Northern end of the bay. Modern yacht harbour. Restaurants and terraces around the water. Upscale seafood, evening ambiance, cocktails. Resort-level pricing, 150-300 MAD/head with drink. Marina restaurants keep longer hours, usually take cards. Good for couples, families with young children (table service, high chairs, menus beyond fish). Mezzo Mezzo — Mediterranean/lounge, 120-280 MAD. A step up from port grills, a step below fine dining.

### Hotel / Resort Restaurants — Best for consistency, international options
Major resort hotels employ trained chefs, execute European dishes properly. Buffet breakfasts include international options alongside Moroccan standards. 600+ MAD for a full meal. Consistent quality, fussy-eater-friendly, convenient. Not the place for authentic local flavor or value.

## 5. MUST-TRY FOOD EXPERIENCES (in order of importance)

1. **Grilled sardines at the port** — The single most authentic Agadir food experience. Walk the grill row at the working fishing port north of the beach. Choose your sardines off the ice. Watch them grilled over charcoal. Eat at a plastic table with bread, lemon, cumin salt, and the smell of charcoal in the air. Go at lunch (noon-2pm) when the morning catch has just landed and grills are most active. 20-45 MAD. This is what Agadir actually is — far more honest than any tagine on a tourist menu.

2. **Souk El Had street food crawl** — Morocco's largest covered market. Morning visit. Eat your way through: lentil soup (0.70 DH!) at the soup stand, chicken tagine from the clay oven (40-60 DH), orange juice squeezed to order (8-12 DH), pastilla and pastries from the bakery alley (3-5 DH each), msemen with honey. Then shop for argan oil and spices.

3. **Argan oil tasting at a women's cooperative** — Just outside Agadir. See the process from nut to oil, learn culinary vs. cosmetic uses, taste cold-pressed argan oil straight from the stone mill. Buy culinary argan oil (roasted, darker) not cosmetic (raw, lighter) for cooking. Tagine drizzled with argan oil is a distinctively nutty, toasted local flavor.

4. **Amlou breakfast** — The signature Souss breakfast. Spread of roasted almonds, argan oil, and honey on fresh bread. Available at argan cooperatives, breakfast cafés, Souk El Had. Ask for it.

5. **Trid at a Talborjt local restaurant** — Layered flatbread with chicken and lentils, the Soussi specialty. Rarely on tourist menus — ask specifically. The Souss Massa regional dish.

6. **Friday couscous** — The national dish, traditionally a Friday meal. Souss couscous is notable for its variety of vegetables. Order it at a local restaurant on Friday.

7. **A whole grilled fish at the port** — Sole or sea bream, picked by weight, grilled, with bread, salad, lemon. The full port-grill experience. 90-180 MAD by weight. Agree price before cooking.

8. **Shawarma in Talborjt** — The Agadir shawarma has a distinctive Soussi spice blend, spicier than expected. Chicken shawarma with harissa. The workday lunch for most locals. Avenue Hassan II and around the bus station.

9. **A meal at a marina restaurant with a view** — Not the most authentic, but a polished seafood dinner over the yachts. Good for a special night. Mixed seafood platter, whole grilled fish, wine/juice list, tablecloths, reliable hygiene. 150-300 MAD/head.

10. **Mint tea ritual** — The pour-from-height, sugar-laden, fragrant ritual. At a table in El Mechouar Square (Tiznit, on a day trip), at a Corniche café, at a Souk El Had stall. As much about the ritual as the taste.

## 6. PRACTICAL TIPS

- **Eat where locals eat, not where tourists are herded.** Talborjt and Souk El Had are the real Agadir. Corniche and Marina restaurants are 20-40% more expensive for the same fish.
- **Port grills are cash-only.** Marina and Corniche restaurants usually take cards.
- **Always agree the by-weight fish price before it's cooked.** Be politely firm if a stall is vague. Pick stalls busy with Moroccan families for best value and freshness.
- **Lunch is the freshest window for fish** — morning boats deliver by 10am, grills most active noon-2pm.
- **Local eating times differ.** Lunch from 1:30pm onwards. Dinner often 8-10pm. Adjust or you'll find places closed.
- **Avoid tourist-only promenade cafés** — cheaper and better options are just a street away in Talborjt.
- **Busy stalls = fresh food.** Empty stalls at peak hours = avoid.
- **Buy culinary argan oil (roasted, darker)** not cosmetic (raw, lighter) — very different flavor for cooking.
- **For a week's groceries for two: under €50** — groceries very cheap compared to Europe.
- **Agadir food scene is strong on fresh Atlantic seafood and traditional Moroccan cooking; lighter on haute cuisine.** The real quality hides in the neighborhoods inland where locals eat.
- **Sardines are Agadir's identity.** This coastal city has one of the most important sardine ports in the Atlantic. The dish you must not miss is the grilled sardine — not the tagine, not the couscous, not anything with a photo on a tourist menu.
