## 1. AGADIR CITY SIGHTS

### Agadir Beach & Corniche (Corniche de la Plage d'Agadir)
- 6-10km crescent of golden sand, the city's centrepiece. Protected bay, calm waters.
- Paved palm-lined promenade (Corniche) — 4km of walking, cycling, jogging. Cafes, ice-cream kiosks, play areas, public outdoor gym stations (free), benches.
- Open 24h. Best at sunrise (locals have the beach) and sunset (entire city comes out for walks).
- Water 16-22°C (refreshing, not warm). Lifeguards in summer.
- Beach vendors sell grilled corn (5-10 MAD), coconuts (10-15 MAD), roasted peanuts (5 MAD), fresh fruit.
- ~300 days of sunshine/year.
- Most lively section: northern part between Marina and the 'I Love Agadir' sign — bars and restaurants concentrated there.
- Free. Sunbed + umbrella rental: 30-50 MAD/day.
- Parking: large car parks behind beach (10 MAD/hour). Paid in high season; arrive early.

### Agadir Marina (Port de Plaisance)
- Modern waterfront at the northern end of the beach. Yachts, fishing boats, restaurants, cafes, boutiques.
- "Most European-feeling part of Agadir" — sleek, modern, polished. Pleasant contrast to the souk.
- Fish market inside harbour gates — fresh sardines, squid, red snapper at rock-bottom prices. Best lunch spot in the city.
- Launch point for boat trips, fishing excursions, sunset cruises.
- Cable car base station nearby (to Kasbah).
- Free parking in marina car park (turn off Boulevard Hassan II at the Port sign).
- Cafes, ice-cream shops for beach-day treat. Good for afternoon coffee with harbour views.

### Agadir Oufella Kasbah (Kasbah of Agadir Oufella)
- 16th-century fortress built in 1540 by Saadian sultan Mohammed ech-Cheikh to protect the city from Portuguese attacks. The Portuguese fort (Santa Cruz do Cabo de Gué) was bombarded and liberated in 1541 using artillery from the kasbah.
- Located on a hill 236m above the bay, north of Agadir near the current port.
- Destroyed in the devastating 1960 earthquake (February 29, 1960 — 20,000-30,000 dead). Only ruins remained: massive wall, the iconic inscription.
- The entrance door bears an authentic inscription in Dutch: "Fear God and respect the King" — a trace of the Dutch settlement in 1746.
- Classified as a historical monument in 1932 (the site) and 1922 (patron saint Sidi Boul Knadel below).
- Extensively restored and reconstructed (opened to public February 2024). The interior underwent archaeological excavations and restoration/restitution work to recreate pre-earthquake sociocultural sequences.
- "The most visited historical monument in Agadir."
- Panoramic views of the bay, the long beach, the marina and port, the white grid of the city, and the Atlantic stretching to the horizon. "Spectacular at sunset." Snow-capped High Atlas mountains glow pink in alpenglow at sunset when facing east.
- The motto carved into the hillside: "God, Country, King" in Arabic, lit at night.
- A place of memory — constantly reminds inhabitants of the terrible night of February 29, 1960.
- Before the 1960 earthquake, the kasbah contained: outer wall with defensive towers, a large mosque, a hospital, a Treasury and Post building, houses/alleys/small courtyards, a "Mellah" (synagogue), important sanctuaries (tomb of Sidi Boujemaa Agnaou of the Gnaoua community, tomb of Lalla Yamna).
- Opening hours: daily 10am-7pm (summer: 10am-8pm; Ramadan: 10am-4:30pm). Last admission 30 min before closing.
- Ticket prices (2024-2026):
  - Free for: 1960 earthquake survivors, accompanying PRM, ICOM/ICOMOS/UNESCO members
  - National visitors & Morocco residents: 10 DH
  - 65 and over: 20 DH
  - Family of 4 (5th member +: 50 DH per additional person): 75 DH/person
  - Group package (10+): additional services 30 DH
  - Audioguide: 20 DH/person
  - Group audio tour: 100 DH
  - Guided tour: 100 DH
- Access: pedestrian path, cable car, shuttle bus, bus, car park.
- On-site services: information points, ticketing, cafe/snack restaurant (coming soon), shops, audioguide (mobile app).
- Official website: www.agadir-oufella.ma

### Agadir Cable Car (Telepherique Agadir)
- First cable car in Morocco. Connects the city of Agadir to its Kasbah.
- Built by Swiss-Austrian group "Garaventa Doppelmayr" (leading specialist in cable transport). Work began July 2021.
- 6-minute trip, 1,700 meters total. Panoramic views of the bay, Atlantic Ocean, city promenade, port, old districts.
- At night, LED lights illuminate the cabins in the sky.
- Second line planned for 2024 (phase 2) linking Agadir Oufella to Danialand amusement park and Africa's largest water park.
- Ticket: ~42.80 USD via Civitatis (includes hotel pickup, English-speaking driver, cable car ticket). 1.5hr duration. Minimum 2 participants.
- Wheelchair accessible. Accessible toilets.

### Mohammed V Mosque (Grande Mosquée Mohammed V)
- Located in Nouveau Talborjt neighbourhood.
- Modern mosque with many round arches, mosaics, and ornaments. Large number of buildings.
- Can only be admired from the outside (photo stop). Impressive exterior.

### Coco Polizzi (Jardin d'Eau / Le Jardin d'Eau)
- Hidden oasis of exotic plants and water gardens.
- Lesser-known gem. Also known as "Le Jardin d'Eau."
- Near the Kasbah. A pleasant contrast to the beach resort.

### Fishing Port (Port de Pêche / Nouveau Port d'Agadir)
- Working harbour, distinct from the Marina (pleasure boats).
- Early morning best (7-8am) to see fishermen and fresh catch being auctioned.
- Fish market with prawns, lobster at fraction of European prices.
- Photography welcome but ask individual fishermen before close-ups.
- Runs 365 days a year. Near the commercial port (not the marina).
- Take a petit taxi from the beach area (10-15 MAD).

### Agadir Beach — specifics
- 6-10km crescent beach. Plage d'Agadir.
- Wide, well-maintained, patrolled by lifeguards in season.
- 29 entrances lead down to the sand from the 4km-long promenade.
- Much of the beach is public (pitch up with towel), but there are also private sections (rent loungers and umbrellas for the day).
- Promenade is raised above the beach, paved — perfect for walking without getting feet sandy.
- Calmest and most swimmable from late spring through early autumn.
- Families wanting pools and shallow supervised swimming often base themselves at a resort.
- The south end near the marina is calmer for swimming.
- Red-flagged areas for water sports.

## 2. MARKETS & SHOPPING

### Souk El Had (the main market)
- One of the largest markets in North Africa / southern Morocco.
- ~6,000 stalls (some sources say 3,000 shops) across 13 hectares (130,000 m²), walled compound.
- Organized by section: spices, argan oil, Berber jewellery, leather goods, ceramics, fresh produce, clothing, household goods, appliances, electronics.
- Open daily except Monday. Best days: Wednesday and Sunday (historical "market days" — busiest). Hours: 6am-8pm (or 8am-8pm depending on source). Mornings for produce, afternoons for shopping.
- Entry: 1 MAD. Located in Hay Mohammadi neighbourhood, 2km from the beach. Taxi from beach: 10-15 MAD. Enter through Gate 1 (main entrance) for most straightforward navigation.
- Primarily a LOCAL market — this is where Agadir shops, not where it sells to tourists. Shopkeepers are relaxed, prices start fair, bargaining is gentle. Atmosphere is intense but not aggressive.
- Argan oil is Agadir's signature buy: dozens of shops sell cosmetic and culinary argan oil at prices well below Marrakech. Buy from busy stalls with good turnover, or from cooperatives for quality you can trust.
- Other buys: Souss saffron, spices, black soap, Mejhoul dates.
- Skip: "Berber" rugs from the first tout.
- The argan oil section is the main draw for visitors.
- The produce section features stunning displays of Souss Valley agriculture — oranges, tomatoes, dates, seasonal fruit.
- Avoid "Berber" rugs from the first tout.
- Near the commercial port (not the marina).
- Can be overwhelming — pick one or two sections to focus on rather than trying to see everything. Allow 2-3 hours.
- For food: cooked-food stalls in the centre of the market are the cheapest lunch in Agadir (tagine 20-30 MAD with bread and tea, or 40-60 DH for chicken tagine made in a dusty clay oven). Soup stand in Souk centre: lentil soup 0.70 DH (!), served with hard bread for dunking. Fresh juices: orange 8-12 DH (look for guys squeezing as you watch, avoid pre-prepared). Cane sugar as alternative. Sweets corner: pastilla and pastries scattered through bakery alley (3-5 DH each, easy to snack and run).

### Argan Oil Cooperatives
- Women's cooperatives along the road between Agadir and Essaouira. The road passes through the heart of argan country.
- Visit in the morning when the women are working.
- Organized tours from 150-300 MAD/person include hotel pickup and a stop at a cooperative.
- Taxi to nearest cooperative: 20-40 MAD round trip (driver waits).
- See the full process: goats in the trees (they eat the argan fruit), manual nut-cracking, stone-grinding, oil pressing.
- Then sit down to a breakfast showcasing argan products: fresh bread with argan oil (nuttier and richer than olive oil), amlou (argan-almond-honey spread), honey from surrounding forest, mint tea.
- Argan oil prices: culinary 60-100 MAD/liter; cosmetic varies. Amlou (argan-almond-honey paste): 50-100 MAD/jar. Argan soap: 10-20 MAD (cooperative direct) or 20-40 MAD (souk).
- IMPORTANT: Buy culinary argan oil (roasted, darker colour) for cooking, NOT cosmetic (raw, lighter) — very different flavour.
- The women producers earn a fair wage. Cooperatives are a good-value, authentic experience.
- Best visited in the morning when the women are working.

### Inezgane Sunday Market
- Largest weekly market in the Souss region.
- Inezgane is the large town adjacent to Agadir.
- Open Sundays, 6am-2pm.
- Vast, sprawling open-air event drawing thousands of buyers and sellers from across southern Morocco.
- Divided into sections: livestock (donkeys, sheep, goats, chickens), produce (mountains of seasonal fruit and vegetables from Souss Valley), clothing, household goods, spices, cosmetics, and a massive secondhand section.
- Worth the trip for the atmosphere alone.

### Marina Shopping
- Modern waterfront development with shops: international fashion brands, swimwear, jewelry, and gift shops selling packaged argan products, ceramics, leather at fixed (slightly elevated) prices.
- Beach boulevard runs parallel to the main beach: hotels, restaurants, ground-floor shops selling beachwear, souvenirs, leather goods, Moroccan crafts.

## 3. NATURAL ATTRACTIONS

### Paradise Valley (Vallée du Paradis / Vallee du Paradis)
- THE standout natural site near Agadir. One of the most photographed natural sites in the Souss-Massa region.
- A palm-lined gorge carved by the Tamraght River through the Anti-Atlas foothills. Series of natural turquoise pools, waterfalls, date palms, oleander, wild fig, carob trees, smooth pink/ochre boulders.
- Location: Tamraght valley, foothills of the Anti-Atlas. Coordinates: approximately 30.587866, -9.531845 (parking); 30.589002, -9.525032 (natural pools). About 35-60 km north of Agadir (sources vary — 35km/1hr or 60km/1hr15). About 800m altitude — cooler than Agadir.
- Distance: roughly 35-60 km north of Agadir, approximately 1 hour by car (some say 40 min). No public transport — need car, grand taxi, or tour.
- Entry: FREE. Parking: 10-20 MAD (sometimes requested by a guardian).
- Time needed: half-day minimum (3-5 hours on site). Full day if hiking upstream to quieter pools. 3-4 hrs is comfortable.
- Best months: April-June and September-October (fullest pools March-June). April, May, June, September, October are best. Spring (March-May) delivers fullest, most swimmable pools after winter rain. Autumn also good. July-August fine but crowded (especially weekends with Agadir families). Winter pools cold but often fuller.
- Water temperature: cold even in summer (fed by mountain springs) — refreshing rather than chilly once in. 16-22°C in winter/spring.
- The pools: 3 tiers connected by hiking paths along the canyon floor.
  - Lower pool: easiest access, closest to parking, most crowded, 3m cliff jump, good for families. ~10-15m wide, 2-3m deep in spring.
  - Middle pool: 20-min hike upstream, turquoise water, dramatic overhanging rock. Near-complete solitude if you hike 15 min further.
  - Upper pool: 45-min hike, virtually empty. "Secrets locals know."
- Trail: roughly 2km total (some sources say ~350m elevation gain, 2km trail). Easy to moderate. Rock-hopping along riverbed, wading through knee-deep water in places, scrambling over boulders. From parking, 15-20 min descent to canyon floor, 25-30 min back up.
- Hike further than the crowd: most visitors stop at first or second pool. Walk 15 minutes further and you'll find near-private pools, often deeper.
- Arrive before 10am — before 9am on weekdays and you'll have the first pools entirely to yourself. Tour groups roll in around 11am-noon. Weekend = packed (local Moroccan families, especially in summer). Tuesday-Thursday = peaceful.
- Don't bring: drones (banned), glass bottles, Bluetooth speakers (respect the site).
- Bring: water shoes or sandals with grip (rocks at pool edges are slippery), swimwear, quick-dry towel, warm layer (valley is noticeably cooler than Agadir), sunscreen and hat (descent offers no shade), plenty of water, small daypack, cash (parking, snacks, optional tip for local guides ~50 MAD).
- Optional: hire a local guide (tip ~50 MAD). Not required — the route is straightforward and signposted.
- Getting there:
  - By car (best option): Take N8 north from Agadir towards Immouzzer des Ida Outanane. After ~30-35 km, follow signs to "Vallée du Paradis" or "Paradise Valley." Trailhead car park is signposted. Google Maps works for main road; the valley track is not consistently mapped. Self-drive is straightforward to the lower parking area; final stretch is winding.
  - By grand taxi: Grand taxis run from Agadir to Imouzzer (around 60-80 MAD/seat, 1.5 hours). From Imouzzer, the valley pool descent is a 30-minute walk.
  - Guided tour: ~€46 (5 hours, includes round-trip transport, guide who knows best swimming spots, traditional Berber lunch with local family). From Agadir or accommodation pickup.
  - Private driver: ~1,040 MAD for 8-hour day trip, 60km, departs 9:00 AM, free cancellation.
- Combos:
  - Imouzzer Ida ou Tanane (Berber village, 15 km higher in the mountain) — for a full day with winter waterfalls, panoramic views across the Sous plain. Waterfall (Cascade du Voile de la Mariée — Bridal Veil Falls) drops ~40m over travertine cliff. Below: extraordinarily lush green oasis.
  - Taghazout (on the way back, 25 min on the coast) — lunch or sunset by the ocean.
  - Aourir and Tamraght (surf villages on the coast) — pleasant coffee stop on the way.
  - Some tours incorporate a stop in the Imouzzer area above the valley, where a small waterfall (seasonal) and Berber villages make for a pleasant extension.
- Lunch: if arranged with a tour, typically served at a local auberge or restaurant in the valley. Some tours include honey tasting in Imouzzer.
- The contrast is the signature: surrounded by dry, stony hillsides typical of southern Morocco, the valley floor is a lush ribbon of green — towering date palms, wild fig trees, oleander bushes, carob trees shading a series of natural pools where the river has carved basins into smooth bedrock. Pools range from shallow sun-warmed paddling spots to deep plunge pools of startling turquoise clarity.
- Local teenagers dive from the rock ledges above.
- The name "Paradise Valley" appears on every Agadir day trip list — but relative inaccessibility (need car, willingness to navigate increasingly narrow road through village of Imouzzer des Ida Outanane) keeps it genuinely pleasant. Crowds manageable outside school holiday weeks. Upper canyon above main tourist pools remains virtually empty.

### Dinosaur Footprints at Anza Beach
- 200+ tridactyl (three digits pointing forward) dinosaur footprints on the seafront.
- Located north of Agadir, a few kilometers from the city center, in the town of Anza.
- Free. Entry points at Rokein Reserve.
- A surprising and educational natural attraction.

### Souss Massa National Park (Parc National Souss-Massa)
- 33,800 hectares of coastal habitat south of Agadir.
- Protects Dorcas gazelle, Addax, North African ostriches, flamingos (at Rivière Massa / Sidi Binzarne — bird watching).
- Ibis Trail hike: easy-moderate, 3-4 hours, 10km, 100m gain. Grand taxi to Massa (30 MAD, 45 min).
- Rivière Massa / Sidi Binzarne: bird watching. Flamingos.
- Entry by grand taxi to Massa (30 MAD, 45 min). The park has multiple entry points.
- Part of the "Safaris et Randonnées" / nature tourism in the Souss-Massa region.
- Also known for antelopes and ostriches (Rokein Reserve — Saharan Fauna: Oryx, Addax, Dorcas Gazelle, North African Ostriches).

### Other nature / coastal spots (see Day Trips)
- Legzira Beach: red stone arches carved by Atlantic erosion. Wild, windy, unspoiled. South of Tiznit.
- Mirleft: coastal town between Tiznit and Sidi Ifni.
- Tifnit: fishing village 30 km south of Agadir, on the Atlantic coast. Mini Sahara dunes nearby.

## 4. FAMILY ATTRACTIONS

### Crocoparc Agadir (Crocodile Park)
- Crocodile zoological park in the town of Drarga, on the outskirts of Agadir, along National Highway 8 (Agadir-Marrakech highway), RN 8 - PK 16 - BP 246, 80046 Drarga Agadir.
- Opened May 31, 2015. Land area: 4 hectares (9.9 acres).
- Home to 325 Nile crocodiles (Crocodylus niloticus) — some over 4 metres long, 80-150kg. Also: giant tortoises (Centrochelys sulcata), green iguanas, giant pythons, anacondas, marmosets.
- Multiple botanical gardens housing plant species from all over the world (around 4 hectares, a "fantastic botanical garden").
- New attraction (July 2025): Morocco's FIRST zipline over live crocodile enclosures — "heart-racing," bird's-eye view of crocodile enclosures while suspended in harnesses above the animals' habitat. Safety measures in place: regular equipment checks, trained staff supervision.
- Baby crocodile nursery (many baby crocodiles already born).
- Games, obstacle courses, treasure cave for children.
- Giant crocodile-shaped building entrance — "you can't miss it."
- Restaurant, snack bar, several dining spaces, "Cave of Treasures" (leisure space), snack restaurant, high chairs available, microwave for baby bottles. Snacks available on site (no authorized picnic).
- Two children's playgrounds. Several resting areas around the circuit.
- Parking near the park, with parking spaces reserved for disabled people.
- Full handicapped access, as well as for families with pushchairs.
- Shop open all year.
- Public conveniences with baby facilities.
- Pets prohibited (health and safety).
- Crocoparc by Night (evening attraction).
- "La Table du Croco" — on-site restaurant.
- Opening hours: Open 7/7 days, 364 days/year. Every day from 10:00 am to 8:00 pm (ticket office closes 30 min before park closure). Hours change according to seasons. During Ramadan: 10am-5pm. Crocodile feeding times change depending on temperature (summer: up to 5 times/day; winter: crocodiles can go more than 3 months without feeding). Closed on 1st day of Aid El Kebir (2nd day: park open 10:00am-6:00pm).
- Ticket prices (2024-2026):
  - Individual Adults: 100 DH
  - Children: 70 DH (age 3 to 12 years)
  - VIP cards (with photo) — Unlimited access to the park for one year: Adults 199 DH, Children 149 DH. Benefits: unlimited access for one year, good plans for VIP card holders, invitations to private events reserved for VIP community.
- Access:
  - Public transport: Bus lines 5 and 9 of the company ALSA go to Drarga (across the municipality of Agadir); another bus from Inezgane. More info on ALSA website.
  - Crocoparc Shuttle bus: Departure to the Park on certain days at 9:30am and 2:45pm from Uniprix Agadir (see ticket prices for excursion; call to reserve). Shuttle can also pick up at hotel on request — call Crocoparc on 05 28 29 79 31.
  - By road: From the map on their website, visualize precisely the location and edit a route from your starting point.
  - Driving time: ~20 minutes by car from the Corniche d'Agadir.
- Phone: +212 (0)5 28 29 79 31. Email: contact@crocoparc.com. Website: www.crocoparc.com.
- Organized tours from Agadir: available via musement (~€23, 2-4hr, includes transport + entry), Civitatis (~€40, 3hr, hotel pickup, bus transport, entry), various operators.
- Reviews: "Incredible, a unique place." Family-friendly, emotional, educational. Some feedback about transport coordination issues (guide not communicating pickup time clearly).

## 5. DAY TRIPS

### Taroudant ("Little Marrakech")
- 80 km east of Agadir. ~1 hour by car or shared taxi (60 MAD/person).
- Intact 7.5 km ochre ramparts (16th century), lively souks, peaceful alleys — "exactly what you imagine of Morocco before mass tourism, without Marrakech's tourist hustle."
- Known as "Little Marrakech" or "Grandmother of Marrakech."
- Best on Tuesday or Sunday (market days). Full day recommended; overnight even better — "the medina at 8am with no tourists is worth the detour."
- Travel: 1hr by shared taxi (60 MAD/person) or car. No train.
- Travel time from Agadir airport: ~1hr by car.
- Good base: Agadir for day trip; Taghazout too.

### Tiznit ("Silver City")
- 90-95 km south of Agadir. ~1 hour 15 min to 1 hour 40 min by car (road distance 92-95 km). ~80-95 min from Agadir airport.
- Morocco's silver jewelry capital. A small walled city built by Sultan Moulay Hassan I in 1882.
- 5-7 km of pink/ochre ramparts (some sources say 6 km, 36 round adobe towers, 7 km of walls, 9 gates). Built by Sultan Moulay Hassan in 1882 to establish a base to stabilize the unruly south.
- The medina is surrounded by ramparts with historic gates. Most impressive gate: Bab Jdid (southeast). Bab Targa gate (west) overlooks the palm grove. For a complete panorama, climb the southern section of the ramparts in the late afternoon — low-angled light turns the adobe coppery red.
- The silver souk (Joutia): one of the most atmospheric silver souks in the country. Every shop stacked floor-to-ceiling with Berber and Tuareg jewelry — fibulae (brooches), bracelets, rings, pendants, Tuareg crosses, hand of Fatima pendants, Berber daggers (khanjar), amber beads. Much of it made on-site by craftsmen you can watch at work.
- Watch master silversmiths (maalems) at work in workshops — free to watch, tip from 20 MAD, 30 min-1hr. The real magic of Tiznit.
- El Mechouar Square: historic heart of the city — ancient square where the sultan received the allegiance of the tribes. Today a large open space lined with cafés. Order mint tea (10 dirhams) and watch: old people chat, children cycle by, women return from the market.
- La Source Bleue (Aïn Tissimane): sacred spring in the heart of the medina, surrounded by legend — the city grew around it. Sometimes 10 DH entry.
- Thursday weekly market (Souk El Had): if you're there on a Thursday, the weekly market outside the walls is enormous — produce, livestock, crafts, everything in between. 1-2 hours.
- Handicraft Centre (government-operated, on the edge of the medina): showcases the full range of Tiznit artisanal production — silver jewelry, leather goods, woven textiles, carpets, wooden items. Fixed prices, certificate of authenticity, supports local artisans. Excellent place to compare styles and prices before diving into the souk.
- Bargaining: prices vary depending on design, weight, craftsmanship. Start at roughly 40-50% of asking price for tourist-oriented stalls; in established workshops with marked prices, bargaining is more modest. Ask artisans to explain the symbolism of the designs.
- Recommended time in Tiznit: 1.5-2 hours, then head back to Agadir on the N1 coastal road.
- Best time to visit: October-April; Thursday (market day).
- Combos: Legzira Beach (red stone arches, ~40 min south of Tiznit), Mirleft, Sidi Ifni, Anti-Atlas coast.
- Transport from Agadir:
  - Private taxi: 850-1,100 MAD, ~1hr20, door-to-door. Top recommendation for most travelers.
  - Bus: ~2hr15, ~50 DH.
  - Grand taxi: 35-50 DH.
  - Car hire: easy — smooth, well-maintained road south on N1 across Souss plain.
- Tiznit is the gateway to the deep south — the road to Mirleft, Sidi Ifni, and the desert beyond all starts here.

### Essaouira
- 175 km north of Agadir. ~2 hours by car on A7/N1 highway.
- Atlantic coastal medina, windy, famous for kitesurfing. Fortress, port, artists, fish restaurants at the harbour.
- Best for: coastal contrast to Agadir, atmospheric medina, fresh grilled fish at harbour (whole fish 90-150 MAD).
- Tight for a day trip: 4 hours driving return, ~4 hours on the ground. Better with overnight.
- Self-drive: A7 and N1 connect Agadir to Essaouira directly — comfortable motorway for 1.5 hours, then 30 minutes on the coastal road. Paid parking outside the medina walls. Total driving: 4 hours return. Leaves ~4 hours on the ground — enough but tight.

### Taghazout
- 21 km north of Agadir. ~25 min by car.
- Surf beach and village. Blue-and-white fishing boats, morning catch inspected by villagers on the shoreline.
- Getting there: Bus 14 (irregular throughout the day, 15 DH) from the main local bus station (next to Agadir's long-distance grand taxi rank, on the southern edge of the city, corner of rue de Fes and rue d'Essaouira). Grands taxis (25 DH) departing for Essaouira will stop at Taghazout. Charter a grands taxi for the day (400 DH).
- Already covered in surf spots — included here for completeness.

### Tafraoute (Anti-Atlas)
- 2.5 hours into the Anti-Atlas mountains.
- Famous for: grandiose rock formations (ombryllus — rounded granite boulders), painted rocks (artist Jean Vernoix painted large white circles and diamonds on the rocks in the 1950s), Berber villages, grand scenery, almond blossom in spring.
- Day trip feasible but long. Paradise Valley pairs well with Imouzzer Ida ou Tanane en route to Tafraoute.
- Paragliding sites around Tafraoute (Col du Tizi'n'Test — thermal site with large vertical drop, superb view of Souss valley).
- From Tafraoute: anticlinal rock formations, vallée du Bani, pic de Tikjda.

### Legzira Beach
- Famous red stone arches carved by Atlantic erosion. Two massive natural arches. Wild, windy, unspoiled by development — dramatically different from Agadir's manicured resort strips.
- South of Tiznit (~40 min). Combined Tiznit + Legzira day trip: 11 hours, ~1,560 MAD private driver, 150km, departs 8:00 AM.
- Sunset photography: the red rocks glow intensely in late afternoon light. Walk the beach to see multiple arches and sea caves.
- Return to Agadir late evening.

### Mirleft
- Quiet coastal town between Tiznit and Sidi Ifni. Surf-friendly, Anti-Atlas coastal scenery. Less touristy than Taghazout.

### Tifnit
- Fishing village 30 km south of Agadir on the Atlantic coast. Mini Sahara dunes nearby. Small desert coast, fishing boats. Stop on the way to Tiznit.

### Agadir → Tafraoute road trip
- Via Fifilou gap (crossing the gap at Fifilou — check wind conditions, alternative launch for experts). Take dirt road to camping 'Terre D'Ocean' in Taghazout (25km north of Agadir). Paragliding takeoff (altitude 283m, easy but not big, seabreeze can be strong).
- Anti-Atlas landscapes, winding roads, grand scenery. 1-3 days for a full road trip.

### Paradise Valley + Imouzzer Ida ou Tanane (combined day)
- Paradise Valley (35-60km north, natural pools) + Imouzzer Ida ou Tanane (Berber village 15km higher in the mountain, 1,180m altitude, views across Sous plain, waterfall that flows in winter and spring, honey tasting).
- Full day. Tour includes transport, guided walk in canyon, swimming time, lunch at local restaurant, sometimes honey tasting in Imouzzer.

## 6. OTHER CITY FEATURES & CONTEXT

### Agadir Beach Promenade (Corniche) activities
- Walking, jogging, cycling (bike/scooter rental), public outdoor gym stations (free — pull-up bars, balance beams, step platforms, well-maintained), beach volleyball, football on hard sand.
- Sunset walks are a daily ritual for locals — "sacred evening ritual." Families with picnic blankets, couples strolling, vendors making final sales. Beach vendors sell grilled corn — "perfect sunset snack."
- Atlantic sunsets are genuinely spectacular.
- Best sunset angle: beach directly below the Kasbah — the hill catches the last light.
- Transition from sunset to Kasbah's illumination on the hill above is a "magical free experience."
- Well lit and pleasant to walk after dark — part of the appeal of a city largely rebuilt after the 1960 earthquake.

### Fishing Port vs Marina
- Two distinct ports:
  - Fishing Port (Port de Pêche / Nouveau Port d'Agadir): working harbour, early morning fish auction, grilled fish restaurants, chaotic, authentic, cash only. Where to eat the cheapest, freshest fish.
  - Marina (Port de Plaisance): pleasure boats, yachts, restaurants, cafes, boutiques, polished, modern, table service, cards accepted.

### 1960 Earthquake context
- February 29, 1960: devastating earthquake destroyed Agadir (magnitude 5.7-5.9, but shallow depth and poor construction caused massive destruction). 20,000-30,000 dead (sources vary). The city was essentially wiped out.
- Modern Agadir was rebuilt as a planned resort city: wide streets, grid layout, modern architecture. No ancient medina (unlike Marrakech, Fes, Taroudant).
- This is important context for visitors expecting "old Morocco." Agadir is modern, open, relaxed — a beach resort, not a medieval city.
- The Agadir Oufella Kasbah is the one place that still speaks of the older, pre-earthquake Agadir.
- Agadir celebrates the anniversary of the earthquake (February 29) and Yennayer (Amazigh New Year, January 13) at the Kasbah.

### Agadir's reputation
- "Agadir has a bad reputation — mostly from travelers who spent three days at an all-inclusive resort and never left it."
- The city and its surroundings hide experiences you won't find anywhere else: world-class surf 25 minutes away, a 16th-century medina an hour up the road (Taroudant).
- Two food scenes: tourist-facing beachfront, and the real Agadir where locals eat (Talborjt, Souk El Had area).

### Agadir at a glance
- Location: southern Atlantic coast of Morocco, 540 km south of Casablanca.
- Climate: 22°C average, 300+ days of sunshine per year.
- Airport: Agadir Al Massira (AGA), 22 km southeast of the city.
- Useful distances: Marrakech 250 km · Taroudant 80 km · Essaouira 175 km · Tiznit 95 km.
- How long to stay: Two full days for the city; three or four with a day trip.
- Best base for first visit: Beachfront or Founty for convenience; Talborjt for more city life.
- Main experiences: Beach, promenade, Souk El Had, Agadir Oufella, food, coastal excursions.
- Car required? No for the city; useful only for independent regional exploring.
- Important expectation: Agadir is modern and spread out, not a walk-everywhere old medina.

## 7. PRACTICAL TIPS

- **Kasbah best time:** Sunset. The golden hour transforms the city below into a sea of shimmering white buildings. Also early morning for High Atlas views (clearest before heat haze builds).
- **Cable car:** Book in advance if possible (minimum 2 participants). Hotel pickup often included. Free cancellation up to 24 hours before.
- **Souk El Had:** Go in the morning for best atmosphere and freshness. Don't try to see everything. Pick 1-2 sections. Cash only. Closed Mondays. Best days: Wednesday and Sunday.
- **Argan oil:** Buy from cooperatives for quality. Culinary (roasted, darker) for cooking, cosmetic (raw, lighter) for skincare. Avoid "Berber" rugs from the first tout.
- **Paradise Valley:** Arrive before 10am. Weekdays best (Tuesday-Thursday). Bring water shoes, sunscreen, warm layer, cash. No drones. Car needed (no public transport). 3-5 hours on site.
- **Day trips:** Taroudant and Tiznit are the most rewarding day trips from Agadir (80km and 95km, easy driving, authentic walled cities). Essaouira is better with an overnight. Tafraoute is a long day or multi-day road trip.
- **Tiznit silver:** Bargain politely (start 40-50% of asking for tourist stalls). Ask artisans to explain the symbolism. The Handicraft Centre offers fixed prices and certificates.
- **Crocoparc:** ~20 min from Corniche. Book online or buy at entrance. Children 3-12: 70 DH, adults 100 DH. VIP card 199 DH for unlimited year access. Check opening hours for Ramadan (10am-5pm). Bus lines 5 & 9 or shuttle bus from Uniprix Agadir.
- **Beach:** Swim where lifeguards position themselves (after 9am). Atlantic currents stronger than they look. Red-flagged areas for water sports.
- **Agadir is not Marrakech beside the sea.** It's a modern Atlantic city built around a broad beach, an easy promenade, and the everyday life of the Souss region. Good for "Morocco without filling every hour."
